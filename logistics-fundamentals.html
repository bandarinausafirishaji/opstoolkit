Logistics Fundamentals

Learn the basic concepts, processes and principles of logistics operations.

1. What Is Logistics?

Logistics is the planning, coordination, movement, storage and control of goods, materials and related information from the point of origin to the point of consumption.

The main objective of logistics is to ensure that the right cargo reaches the right place, at the right time, in the right condition and at an appropriate cost.

2. Key Logistics Activities

Logistics involves several interconnected activities that support the movement and management of cargo.

  • Transportation and cargo movement
  • Warehousing and storage
  • Cargo handling
  • Inventory management
  • Order processing
  • Documentation and information flow
  • Loading and unloading
  • Delivery coordination
  • Packaging and cargo protection
  • Tracking and operational reporting

3. Cargo Movement

Cargo movement refers to the physical transportation of goods between different locations. The movement may involve several stages depending on the supply chain and transport requirements.

Typical Cargo Flow:
Supplier → Origin Warehouse → Transport → Port / Terminal → Destination Transport → Warehouse → Final Customer

Effective cargo movement requires proper planning, documentation, equipment, communication and coordination between the parties involved.

4. Transport Systems

Different modes of transport are used depending on cargo type, distance, cost, urgency and operational requirements.

Road Transport

Road transport is commonly used for local distribution, first-mile and last-mile delivery, inland cargo movement and container transport between ports, yards, warehouses and customers.

Maritime Transport

Maritime transport is widely used for international and regional cargo movement, particularly for containers, bulk cargo and project cargo.

Rail Transport

Rail transport can support the movement of large cargo volumes over long inland distances and can be integrated with port and warehouse operations.

Air Transport

Air transport is generally used where speed is important, particularly for urgent, high-value or time-sensitive cargo.

5. Warehousing

Warehousing is the organized storage and management of goods before they are moved to the next stage of the supply chain.

Typical warehouse activities include receiving, inspection, recording, put-away, storage, picking, packing, dispatch and inventory control.

6. Supply Chain

A supply chain is the broader network of organizations, people, resources, processes and systems involved in producing, moving, storing and delivering goods to the final customer.

Logistics is an important part of supply chain management because it focuses heavily on the movement and storage of goods and the related flow of information.

7. Ports and Terminals

Ports and terminals are important points in the movement of cargo, especially for international trade. Cargo may be received, inspected, stored, handled, transferred or released through port and terminal facilities.

For containerized cargo, common operational areas include the berth, container yard, gate, inspection areas and related cargo handling zones.

8. Logistics Documentation

Documentation provides the information required to identify, authorize, track and control cargo movement.

Examples of logistics-related documents include transport documents, delivery notes, cargo manifests, invoices, packing lists, release documents and proof of delivery records.

Accurate documentation helps reduce delays, errors, disputes and unnecessary operational costs.

9. Logistics Coordination

Logistics operations involve coordination between multiple parties. Depending on the operation, these may include suppliers, customers, transporters, warehouses, ports, terminal operators, customs agents, freight forwarders and other service providers.

Good coordination requires clear communication, accurate information, timely updates and defined responsibilities.

10. Key Logistics Performance Areas

Logistics performance can be monitored through several operational areas, including:

  • On-time delivery
  • Cargo condition
  • Transport utilization
  • Equipment utilization
  • Warehouse efficiency
  • Inventory accuracy
  • Documentation accuracy
  • Operational cost
  • Safety and compliance

11. Practical Logistics Principle

A successful logistics operation depends on balancing service, cost, time, safety and cargo condition. A faster operation is not necessarily efficient if it creates unnecessary cost, damage, safety risks or documentation problems.

Therefore, logistics professionals should focus on controlled, coordinated and measurable operations rather than simply moving cargo from one point to another.
