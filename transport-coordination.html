Transport Coordination

Practical guide to truck planning, cargo movement and transport operations.

1. Introduction to Transport Coordination

Transport coordination involves planning, organizing, monitoring and controlling the movement of cargo between different locations.

Effective transport coordination requires proper planning of trucks, drivers, cargo, routes, documentation, loading activities and delivery requirements.

2. Main Transport Coordination Activities

Transport coordination may include the following activities:

  • Transport planning
  • Truck allocation
  • Driver coordination
  • Cargo planning
  • Route planning
  • Dispatch coordination
  • Loading coordination
  • Delivery monitoring
  • Proof of Delivery management
  • Transport reporting

3. Transport Planning

Transport planning should be completed before vehicles are dispatched. The coordinator should understand the cargo requirements, destination, available vehicles and operational schedule.

Key Planning Information

  • Cargo type
  • Cargo quantity
  • Cargo weight and dimensions
  • Origin
  • Destination
  • Required delivery date
  • Vehicle type
  • Driver availability
  • Route requirements
  • Required documentation

4. Truck Allocation

The correct vehicle should be selected according to the cargo characteristics and transport requirements.

Vehicle Type Typical Application
Container Truck Transporting shipping containers.
Flatbed Truck Transporting machinery, pipes, steel and other suitable cargo.
Box Truck Transporting enclosed general cargo.
Tipper Truck Transporting suitable bulk materials.
Lowbed Trailer Transporting heavy or oversized equipment.

5. Driver Coordination

Drivers are an important part of the transport operation. Clear communication and proper instructions should be provided before dispatch.

Important driver information may include:

  • Driver name
  • Contact number
  • Vehicle registration
  • Trailer registration
  • Assigned cargo
  • Loading location
  • Delivery location
  • Required reporting time
  • Special operational instructions

6. Vehicle Readiness

Before dispatch, the vehicle should be checked to confirm that it is suitable and ready for the assigned operation.

  • Vehicle condition
  • Tyres
  • Braking system
  • Lights
  • Mirrors
  • Trailer condition
  • Required safety equipment
  • Load securing equipment
  • Required vehicle documents

7. Cargo Loading Coordination

Loading should be properly coordinated to ensure that cargo is handled safely and loaded according to the transport requirements.

  1. Confirm the cargo and loading instructions.
  2. Position the correct vehicle.
  3. Inspect the vehicle before loading.
  4. Verify cargo quantity and condition.
  5. Load the cargo using suitable equipment.
  6. Distribute the load appropriately.
  7. Secure the cargo where required.
  8. Complete loading documentation.

8. Dispatch Process

A controlled dispatch process helps ensure that vehicles leave the loading location with the correct cargo, documents and instructions.

  1. Confirm vehicle and driver details.
  2. Verify cargo information.
  3. Confirm destination.
  4. Verify required documents.
  5. Check cargo securing.
  6. Record dispatch time.
  7. Provide delivery instructions.
  8. Start transport monitoring.

9. Transport Monitoring

Transport movements should be monitored from dispatch until delivery is completed.

Monitoring may include:

  • Vehicle departure
  • Current location
  • Route progress
  • Operational delays
  • Breakdowns
  • Traffic conditions
  • Delivery arrival
  • Proof of Delivery

Early identification of delays allows the transport coordinator to communicate with relevant stakeholders and take appropriate operational action.

10. Route Planning

Route planning should consider the cargo, vehicle type, road conditions, distance, traffic, restrictions and delivery requirements.

  • Origin and destination
  • Road condition
  • Bridge and height restrictions
  • Weight restrictions
  • Traffic conditions
  • Security considerations
  • Fuel and rest requirements
  • Alternative routes

11. Transport Documentation

Accurate documentation provides operational control and traceability throughout the transport process.

Common transport documents may include:

  • Dispatch Note
  • Delivery Note
  • Waybill
  • Loading Record
  • Vehicle Inspection Record
  • Proof of Delivery (POD)
  • Driver Instructions
  • Incident Report

12. Delivery Coordination

Delivery coordination ensures that cargo reaches the correct destination and is properly received.

  • Confirm delivery location.
  • Confirm receiver availability.
  • Provide estimated arrival information.
  • Coordinate unloading arrangements.
  • Verify delivered cargo.
  • Obtain receiver confirmation.
  • Collect Proof of Delivery.
  • Record any delivery exceptions.

13. Transport Safety

Safety should be maintained throughout the entire transport operation.

  • Use appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).
  • Follow approved loading procedures.
  • Secure cargo correctly.
  • Observe applicable road safety requirements.
  • Do not exceed vehicle or trailer capacity.
  • Monitor driver working and rest requirements.
  • Report accidents and incidents immediately.
  • Follow approved emergency procedures.

14. Handling Transport Delays

Transport delays may occur because of traffic, vehicle breakdowns, weather, loading problems, documentation issues or other operational conditions.

When a delay occurs, the coordinator should identify the cause, communicate the situation to relevant stakeholders, assess the impact and coordinate the appropriate response.

15. Transport Performance Monitoring

Transport performance can be monitored using operational indicators such as:

  • On-time delivery rate
  • Truck turnaround time
  • Vehicle utilization
  • Delivery completion rate
  • Transport cost
  • Fuel consumption
  • Number of delays
  • Accidents and incidents
  • Cargo damage incidents

16. Practical Operational Principle

Effective transport coordination depends on proper planning, clear communication, suitable vehicles, accurate documentation, continuous monitoring and timely delivery.

Before dispatch, confirm the cargo, vehicle, driver, documents, route and destination. During the movement, monitor progress and communicate operational changes promptly.
