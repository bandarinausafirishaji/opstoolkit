Port & Terminal Operations

Practical guide to port, terminal and container handling operations.

1. Introduction to Port & Terminal Operations

Port and terminal operations involve the coordinated activities required to receive, handle, store, transfer and release cargo through ports and terminal facilities.

Effective terminal operations require coordination between vessels, terminal operators, yard teams, gate operations, equipment operators, transporters, shipping lines, cargo owners and relevant authorities.

2. Main Areas of a Port and Terminal

The main operational areas of a port or terminal may include:

  • Berth: The designated area where a vessel is positioned for cargo operations.
  • Quay: The waterfront structure used to accommodate vessels and cargo handling operations.
  • Container Yard (CY): The designated area where containers are temporarily stored and organized.
  • Gate: The controlled entry and exit point for trucks, containers and other cargo movements.
  • Inspection Area: A designated area for cargo or container inspection.
  • Warehouse: A facility used for storing cargo that requires covered or controlled storage.
  • Equipment Area: An area supporting cargo handling and terminal equipment operations.

3. Berth Operations

Berth operations cover the activities performed while a vessel is positioned at the berth.

  • Vessel arrival coordination
  • Berth allocation
  • Mooring operations
  • Loading and discharge planning
  • Cargo handling
  • Equipment coordination
  • Vessel departure preparation

Effective berth coordination aims to reduce vessel turnaround time while maintaining safe and controlled cargo operations.

4. Container Yard Operations

The Container Yard (CY) is the area where containers are stored temporarily before their next stage of movement.

Key Yard Activities

  • Container receiving
  • Container stacking
  • Container shifting
  • Container inspection
  • Container release
  • Container dispatch
  • Yard inventory control

Effective yard planning should consider container type, destination, status, vessel, customer requirements and planned movement.

5. Gate Operations

The terminal gate is a critical control point for monitoring and recording the movement of trucks and containers into and out of the terminal.

Typical Gate Flow

  1. Truck arrival
  2. Document verification
  3. Container number verification
  4. Seal verification
  5. External condition inspection
  6. System confirmation
  7. Gate-in or Gate-out recording

An efficient gate process helps reduce truck turnaround time and maintains accurate records of container movements.

6. Cargo Handling Equipment

Ports and terminals use different types of equipment depending on cargo type, weight, dimensions and operational requirements.

Equipment Typical Use
Reach Stacker Handling, lifting and stacking containers.
Rubber Tyred Gantry Crane (RTG) Stacking and transferring containers within the yard.
Quay Crane Loading and discharging containers between the vessel and quay.
Terminal Tractor Transporting containers within the terminal.
Forklift Handling cargo within the rated lifting capacity of the equipment.

7. Container Handling Workflow

A typical container movement may follow the workflow below:

  1. Vessel arrival and preparation of the container discharge plan.
  2. Container discharge from the vessel.
  3. Transfer of the container to the yard.
  4. Assignment of a yard position.
  5. Container storage and system tracking.
  6. Release authorization verification.
  7. Truck arrival at the terminal.
  8. Container loading onto the truck.
  9. Gate-out completion.

8. Container Inspection

Container inspection helps verify the physical condition of a container before receiving, release or dispatch.

External Inspection

  • Container number
  • Doors and locking devices
  • Side walls
  • Front wall
  • Roof
  • Corner posts
  • Bottom structure
  • Visible structural damage

Internal Inspection

  • Floor condition
  • Internal walls
  • Roof condition
  • Cleanliness
  • Signs of water leakage
  • Odour
  • Contamination

9. Documentation and Information Flow

Port and terminal operations depend on accurate information and documentation to control cargo and container movements.

Examples of operational documents and information include:

  • Bill of Lading (B/L)
  • Delivery Order (DO)
  • Container Release Information
  • Gate Pass
  • Truck Details
  • Container Number
  • Seal Number
  • Inspection Records
  • Terminal System Records

10. Safety in Terminal Operations

Safety is a fundamental part of port and terminal operations.

  • Use appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).
  • Separate pedestrian areas from equipment operating zones.
  • Operate lifting equipment within approved safe operating limits.
  • Follow terminal traffic management procedures.
  • Report unsafe conditions and operational hazards.
  • Follow approved lifting and cargo handling procedures.
  • Restrict unauthorized access to hazardous operational areas.

11. Terminal Coordination

Effective terminal operations require continuous communication between departments and operational stakeholders.

  • Terminal Operations
  • Yard Planning
  • Gate Operations
  • Equipment Operators
  • Shipping Lines
  • Transporters
  • Cargo Owners
  • Customs and other relevant authorities

Important information such as container status, truck arrival, equipment availability, delays and operational changes should be communicated accurately and on time.

12. Key Terminal Performance Areas

Common operational performance areas include:

  • Vessel Turnaround Time
  • Truck Turnaround Time
  • Yard Utilization
  • Equipment Productivity
  • Container Dwell Time
  • Gate Processing Time
  • Safety Performance
  • Damage Incidents

13. Practical Operational Principle

In port and terminal operations, accurate information, effective coordination, safety and timing are fundamental to operational efficiency.

Before any cargo or container movement takes place, confirm the status, documentation, equipment, personnel and transport arrangements required for the operation.
