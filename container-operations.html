Container Operations

Practical guide to container types, handling, inspection, storage and transport.

1. Introduction to Container Operations

Container operations involve the receiving, inspection, handling, storage, movement and release of shipping containers within ports, terminals, depots, warehouses and transport facilities.

Effective container operations require accurate identification, proper handling procedures, suitable equipment, reliable documentation and coordination between all operational stakeholders.

2. Common Container Types

Different container types are used depending on the nature, dimensions and handling requirements of the cargo.

Container Type Typical Application
20ft Dry Container General cargo requiring standard enclosed container space.
40ft Dry Container General cargo requiring greater internal capacity.
40ft High Cube Cargo requiring additional internal height and volume.
Open Top Container Oversized or high cargo that cannot easily be loaded through standard doors.
Flat Rack Heavy or oversized cargo requiring open sides or ends.
Refrigerated Container (Reefer) Temperature-controlled cargo.

3. Container Identification

Every standard shipping container has an identification number used for operational, documentation and tracking purposes.

A typical container identification consists of:

  • Owner or operator prefix
  • Equipment category identifier
  • Serial number
  • Check digit

The container number should be verified against the relevant documents and terminal or depot system before any movement is completed.

4. Container Inspection

Container inspection is performed to determine the physical condition of a container before receiving, storage, release or dispatch.

External Inspection

  • Container number
  • Doors and locking mechanisms
  • Side walls
  • Front wall
  • Roof
  • Corner posts
  • Understructure
  • Visible structural damage
  • Signs of corrosion

Internal Inspection

  • Floor condition
  • Internal walls
  • Roof condition
  • Cleanliness
  • Water leakage
  • Odour
  • Contamination

5. Container Handling

Containers must be handled using suitable equipment and approved operational procedures.

Common container handling equipment includes:

  • Quay Cranes
  • Reach Stackers
  • Rubber Tyred Gantry Cranes (RTG)
  • Rail Mounted Gantry Cranes (RMG)
  • Terminal Tractors
  • Suitable Forklifts

Equipment selection should consider container weight, dimensions, lifting points, operational environment and manufacturer requirements.

6. Container Yard Operations

The Container Yard (CY) is used for temporary storage and organized management of containers before their next movement.

Key Yard Activities

  • Container receiving
  • Container positioning
  • Stacking
  • Shifting
  • Inspection
  • Inventory control
  • Release preparation
  • Dispatch

Good yard planning should consider container status, destination, equipment requirements, expected movements and available storage space.

7. Container Receiving Process

A typical container receiving process may include the following steps:

  1. Confirm the container identification number.
  2. Verify supporting documentation.
  3. Inspect the external condition.
  4. Verify the seal where applicable.
  5. Record damage or exceptions.
  6. Confirm the receiving location.
  7. Update the relevant system or records.
  8. Position the container in the assigned area.

8. Container Release Process

Before releasing a container, operational personnel should confirm that the required authorization and information are available.

  • Verify container number.
  • Confirm release authorization.
  • Verify truck and driver information.
  • Check seal information where applicable.
  • Inspect container condition.
  • Confirm loading or pickup instructions.
  • Record the release movement.
  • Complete gate-out documentation.

9. Container Damage Recording

Any visible damage identified during container operations should be recorded accurately and reported according to the applicable procedure.

Damage records may include:

  • Container number
  • Date and time
  • Location
  • Damage type
  • Damage location
  • Photographic evidence
  • Inspection findings
  • Recommended action

Accurate damage recording helps maintain proper container condition records and supports operational accountability.

10. Container Transport

Container transport involves moving containers between ports, terminals, depots, warehouses, customer locations and other operational facilities.

Before dispatching a container, verify:

  • Container number
  • Truck suitability
  • Trailer condition
  • Container locking devices
  • Required documents
  • Route and destination
  • Driver details
  • Load security requirements

11. Container Safety

Safety must be maintained throughout the container handling and transport process.

  • Use appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).
  • Keep personnel clear of suspended loads.
  • Use suitable lifting equipment.
  • Follow approved lifting procedures.
  • Inspect equipment before use.
  • Maintain safe working distances.
  • Secure containers correctly during transport.
  • Report unsafe conditions immediately.

12. Documentation and Records

Accurate documentation is essential for controlling container movements and maintaining operational traceability.

Common records include:

  • Container Receiving Form
  • Container Inspection Checklist
  • Container Damage Report
  • Gate-In / Gate-Out Record
  • Release Documentation
  • Delivery Documentation
  • Equipment Records
  • Yard Inventory Records

13. Operational Best Practices

  • Always verify the container number before movement.
  • Record container condition accurately.
  • Maintain updated yard inventory.
  • Use suitable equipment for each operation.
  • Maintain clear communication between operational teams.
  • Keep documentation complete and traceable.
  • Report damage and operational exceptions promptly.
  • Follow approved safety procedures at all times.

14. Practical Operational Principle

Effective container operations depend on accurate identification, proper inspection, safe handling, organized storage, reliable documentation and timely coordination.

Before any container movement takes place, confirm the container status, condition, documentation, equipment, personnel and transport arrangements required for the operation.
